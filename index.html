FROM AN IDEA TO AN APP

I'm Timothy Piao, a high school student. I've always loved two things: sport and building. FitFlow is where they finally met.

01 — INSPIRATION

The problem I kept seeing

I noticed that everyone around me wanted to get in shape but got stuck in the same place. Generic PDF workout plans didn't fit anyone. Friends downloaded fitness apps and quit in a week because they were confusing, or asked for money before they'd done a single push-up. I kept thinking: working out shouldn't require you to already be an expert. What if an app just built the plan for you — and quietly got smarter as you trained?

02 — THE IDEA

One plan that builds itself

The core idea became simple: ask a few questions — your goal, your level, your equipment, how many days you can train — and generate a real, personalized weekly program instantly. No blank screens, no guesswork. Then let that plan progress on its own as the user logs their sessions, so it always feels one step ahead of them instead of one step behind.

03 — DESIGN

Designing for the person who hates the gym

I designed FitFlow for the version of me on day one: nervous, unsure, easily overwhelmed. That's why the workout screen shows exactly one exercise at a time with a demo, auto rest timers, and a big green "next." I chose a calm dark theme with a single energetic green accent, a bold display typeface, and generous spacing so nothing feels cluttered. Every screen answers one question: "what do I do right now?"

04 — BUILDING

Turning mockups into a real product

I built FitFlow as a modern web app so anyone could open it on their phone with no download. A deterministic plan generator picks exercises from a big catalog based on your goal and equipment. I added GPS run tracking with a motion check that confirms you're actually running (not driving), a satellite route map, nutrition tracking with a food database, cloud accounts so your data follows you across devices, and a guided first-time tour so new users never feel lost.

05 — WHAT I LEARNED

Shipping is a skill

The hardest part wasn't any single feature — it was the hundred small decisions that make an app feel finished: what happens when there's no data, how errors are handled, whether a stretch shows "reps" or "seconds," how a password reset link should behave. Building FitFlow taught me that great products aren't one big idea; they're a thousand careful details, iterated on until they disappear.
